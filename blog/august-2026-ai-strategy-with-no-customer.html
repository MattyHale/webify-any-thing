August 2026: The AI Strategy With No Customer

Reviewed and revised: August 2026

Satellite operators are under pressure to tell a bigger story.

Building satellites is expensive. Launching them is expensive. Operating them is expensive. Revenue takes time, investors want growth, and another funding round is often never far away.

So the industry has found its new favourite word: AI.

Every satellite company is suddenly becoming an AI company. Every platform is adding an AI assistant. Every data provider is connecting to a Model Context Protocol server and announcing that its imagery can now be accessed by agents.

It sounds like progress. It looks good in an investor presentation. It gives the company a new announcement to make.

But who is the customer?

Who is the end user?

What problem are they actually paying to solve?

Connecting satellite data to an MCP does not create demand. It does not explain why a mining company, defence analyst, insurer, farmer or government agency would change the way they work. It does not remove procurement barriers, improve data quality, reduce delivery times or make satellite imagery easier to buy.

It simply gives an AI system another source it can theoretically access.

That distinction matters.

There is a growing risk that parts of the space industry are building for the AI bubble rather than building for customers. The technology is being designed backwards from the investor narrative:

  1. Investors want AI exposure.
  2. The company needs more capital.
  3. The product is repositioned as AI infrastructure.
  4. Customer demand is assumed to follow.

It rarely works that way.

Customers do not wake up wanting an MCP connection. They want to detect a change, investigate an event, monitor an asset, receive data in an existing system or make a decision faster.

The winning satellite companies will not be the ones that mention AI most often. They will be the ones that can clearly answer three questions:

Who uses the product?

What decision does it improve?

Why will they pay for it?

Until those answers exist, the AI strategy is not a go-to-market strategy.

It is a fundraising strategy.
